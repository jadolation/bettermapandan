Third-Class Municipality · Region I

A rice-growing town in Pangasinan's third district

Mapandan sits in the Third Congressional District of Pangasinan, bounded by San Jacinto to the north, Mangaldan to the northwest, Manaoag to the east, and Santa Barbara to the south. Covering 30.00 square kilometres, around three-quarters of its land supports agriculture — mainly rice, alongside corn, livestock, and seasonal vegetables. The municipal administration operates under the tagline “Kaakibat niyo ang Aktibo at Responsableng Liderato” (Partnering with you in Active and Responsible Leadership).

01

Founded Dec. 28, 1887

Separated from Mangaldan as an independent pueblo under Governor General Emilio Bravo, led by ten local leaders including Antonio Morales, Sabino Prado, and Andres Bongato.

02

Re-established 1909

A severe epidemic forced temporary re-annexation to Mangaldan. Full autonomy was restored via Executive Order No. 10 by Governor General James F. Smith.

03

Agricultural heart

Approximately 75% of the municipality's 30 km² is dedicated to lowland crop production, livestock, and poultry farming.

A Brief History

From barrio to independent town

Dec. 28, 1887

Mapandan is created as an independent pueblo through Director General No. 39, Administration Civil No. 169-C, signed by Governor General Emilio Bravo. Ten local leaders — Antonio Morales, Sabino Prado, Andres Bongato, Ambrosio Calimlim, Valeriano Tamondong, Tomas Aquino, Fulgencio Nato, Filomeno Sarmiento, Jose Lalas, and Florencio Datuin — led the petition.

1887–1890s

The new town originally comprises the southern barrios of Payapay, Baloling, Apaya, and Amanoaoac. Its administrative seat is first set up in Barrio Torres, then relocated to present-day Poblacion on land donated by the Aquino family.

1903

A severe epidemic devastates the municipality, causing substantial loss of life, population migration, and fiscal insolvency.

1905–1908

Due to financial insolvency, Mapandan temporarily surrenders its municipal status and is re-absorbed as part of Mangaldan.

1909

Mapandan is re-established as an independent municipality under Executive Order No. 10, issued by Governor General James F. Smith. The town was named for the abundance of fragrant pandan plants in the area.

2024–2025

Inauguration of the Super Health Center Phase 1 and integration into the national eGovPH digital service system.

Cultural Heritage

Local traditions and community life

Pandan Festival

Held annually during the second week of April, celebrating the town's agricultural harvest and its namesake plant, Pandanus amaryllifolius. Events include street-dancing pageants, trade fairs, and exhibitions of traditional pandan leaf weaving.

Town Plaza

Located in Poblacion, the Mapandan Town Plaza is widely regarded as one of Pangasinan's best-maintained civic parks, featuring an open-air auditorium, tennis courts, recreational children's playgrounds, and landscaped green space.

Agri-Tourism in Luyan

Barangay Luyan is highlighted in regional DOT farm-to-table culinary circuits, offering agri-tourism programs that showcase sustainable crop cultivation, local gastronomy, and rural life.
