Third-Class Municipality · Region I

A rice-growing town in Pangasinan's third district

Mapandan sits in the Third Congressional District of Pangasinan, bordered by San Jacinto to the north, Mangaldan to the northwest, Manaoag to the east, and Santa Barbara to the south. Around three-quarters of its land supports agriculture — mainly rice, alongside corn, livestock, and seasonal vegetables — and the town marks its harvest each April with the Pandan Festival.

A Brief History

From barrio to independent town

1887

Mapandan is created as an independent pueblo, separating from mother town Mangaldan, through Director General No. 39, Administration Civil No. 169-C, signed by Governor General Emilio Bravo. Ten local leaders — including Antonio Morales, Sabino Prado, and Andres Bongato — led the petition.

1887

The new town originally comprises the southern barrios of Payapay, Baloling, Apaya, and Amanoaoac. Its administrative seat is first set up in Barrio Torres.

Later 1800s

The seat of government moves to its present-day location in Poblacion, following land donations and lot distributions by the Aquino family.

1903–1908

A severe epidemic sharply reduces the population and local tax base, forcing a temporary re-annexation to Mangaldan.

1908

Full municipal autonomy is restored under Executive Order No. 10, issued by Governor General James F. Smith.
