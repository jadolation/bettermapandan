Third-Class Municipality · Region I

A rice-growing town in Pangasinan's third district

Mapandan sits in the Third Congressional District of Pangasinan, bounded by San Jacinto to the north, Mangaldan to the northwest, Manaoag to the east, and Santa Barbara to the south. Covering 30.00 square kilometres, around three-quarters of its land supports agriculture — mainly rice, alongside corn, livestock, and seasonal vegetables. The municipal administration operates under the tagline “Kaakibat niyo ang Aktibo at Responsableng Liderato” (Partnering with you in Active and Responsible Leadership).

01

Founded Dec. 28, 1887

Separated from Mangaldan as an independent pueblo under Governor General Emilio Bravo, led by ten local leaders including Antonio Morales, Sabino Prado, and Andres Bongato.

02

Re-established 1909

A severe epidemic forced temporary re-annexation to Mangaldan. Full autonomy was restored via Executive Order No. 10 by Governor General James F. Smith.

03

Agricultural heart

Approximately 75% of the municipality's 30 km² is dedicated to lowland crop production, livestock, and poultry farming.

A Brief History

The Story of Mapandan

From a new pueblo to a growing community

Before 1887

Southern barrios of Mangaldan

Before Mapandan existed, the area belonged to Mangaldan as southern communities including Payapay, Baloling, Apaya, and Amanoaoac.

Ten local leaders — Antonio Morales, Sabino Prado, Andres Bongato, Ambrosio Calimlim, Valeriano Tamondong, Tomas Aquino, Fulgencio Nato, Filomeno Sarmiento, Jose Lalas, and Florencio Datuin — led the petition for a separate town.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Municipal Profile — Historical Background

Dec. 28, 1887

Mapandan becomes a pueblo

Established as an independent pueblo through Director General No. 39, signed by Governor General Emilio Bravo.

The new pueblo was initially composed of the southern barrios of Mangaldan. Its administrative seat was first set up in Barrio Torres, then relocated to present-day Poblacion on land donated by the Aquino family.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Municipal Profile — Historical Background

1887–1890s

The first town center

The first seat was in Barrio Torres, near a church where Fr. Jose Torres served. The town was named for the abundance of fragrant pandan plants.

The Torres location proved inconvenient — too close to the southern boundary. The center was eventually transferred to an area that had been a sitio of Apaya. Leon Hilario Aquino encouraged settlement by subdividing land into uniform lots.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Barangay Torres History; Barangay Poblacion History

1903

The epidemic

A severe epidemic devastated the municipality, causing population loss, migration, and fiscal insolvency. The 1903 census recorded 4,198 residents.

The epidemic directly affected Mapandan’s ability to function as a municipality, leading to its temporary return to Mangaldan.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Municipal Profile; PSA Census 1903

1905–1908

Return to Mangaldan

Due to financial insolvency, Mapandan temporarily surrendered its municipal status and was re-absorbed as part of Mangaldan.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Municipal Profile — Historical Background

1909

Mapandan rises again

Re-established as an independent municipality under Executive Order No. 10, issued by Governor General James F. Smith.

After years of demographic and financial difficulties, Mapandan was reorganized and re-established as an independent municipality, beginning a new chapter of growth.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Municipal Profile — Historical Background

1940s

War and reconstruction

The Second World War interrupted development across Pangasinan. Postwar years brought land ownership changes and community rebuilding.

In the postwar years, tenants gained opportunities to own land they had been farming. Communities like Sta. Maria developed from hacienda land into independent barrios.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Barangay Sta. Maria History

1965

Sta. Maria becomes a barrio

Formerly a sitio of Luyan on hacienda land, Sta. Maria was established as a separate barrio, marking the growth of Mapandan’s community structure.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Barangay Sta. Maria History

1967

Golden becomes a barangay

Formerly Amanoaoac North, Golden was established as a separate barangay. Named after the Golden Distillery that operated after liberation.

Golden underwent substantial development including road improvement, waterworks, school construction, and cooperative organization. In 1972, it was recognized as a SEATO/PACD Model Village, with the award presented at Malacañang.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Barangay Golden History

1972–1973

Coral is established

Created through Municipal Council Resolution No. 87 (1972), Provincial Board Resolution No. 155, and Proclamation No. 11 (1973). Named after the stone coral enclosure built during the Spanish era.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Barangay Coral History

2002

Third-class municipality

Mapandan was elevated to a third-class municipality, reflecting income and revenue growth under then-Mayor Jose Ferdinand Z. Calimlim Jr.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Physical Characteristics — Historical Development

2010s

The Pandan Festival grows

The Pandan Festival developed into an important annual celebration of Mapandan’s identity, culture, and community, held alongside the town fiesta.

Source: Municipality of Mapandan, Official Website

2022–2024

Expanding healthcare access

National health infrastructure funding supported the Mapandan Super Health Center. Its first phase was inaugurated on March 15, 2024, serving residents of all 15 barangays.

The 2022 General Appropriations Act included &₱circ;6.5 million for the facility. Phase 1 provides laboratory and dental services for Mapandan and nearby municipalities.

Source: Philippine News Agency, March 15, 2024; DBM General Appropriations Act 2022

2024

Mapandan today

More than 130 years after its establishment, Mapandan is a 15-barangay municipality with a population of 38,228, continuing to invest in public services and community development.

Source: Philippine Statistics Authority, 2024 POPCEN

Cultural Heritage

Local traditions and community life

Pandan Festival

Held annually during the second week of April, celebrating the town's agricultural harvest and its namesake plant, Pandanus amaryllifolius. Events include street-dancing pageants, trade fairs, and exhibitions of traditional pandan leaf weaving.

Town Plaza

Located in Poblacion, the Mapandan Town Plaza is widely regarded as one of Pangasinan's best-maintained civic parks, featuring an open-air auditorium, tennis courts, recreational children's playgrounds, and landscaped green space.

Agri-Tourism in Luyan

Barangay Luyan is highlighted in regional DOT farm-to-table culinary circuits, offering agri-tourism programs that showcase sustainable crop cultivation, local gastronomy, and rural life.
